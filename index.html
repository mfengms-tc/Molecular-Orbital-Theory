Quick Recap: What You Just Learned

How Valence Bond Theory Views a Covalent Bond

In Valence Bond (VB) Theory, a covalent bond forms when two half-filled atomic (or hybrid) orbitals on adjacent atoms overlap in space, pairing their two electrons with opposite spins.

Localized Bonds

Shared electrons stay strictly between the two bonded nuclei.

σ and π Overlap

Head-on overlap gives σ bonds; side-by-side overlap gives π bonds.

Hybridization

sp, sp2, sp3 orbitals explain VSEPR 3D shapes nicely.

Module 02

Postulates & Core Principles of MO Theory

Instead of gluing atoms together with localized pairs, MO Theory solves the Schrödinger equation for the entire molecule.

1

Molecular Delocalization

Just as electrons in atoms occupy Atomic Orbitals (AOs) around one nucleus, electrons in molecules occupy Molecular Orbitals (MOs) that spread across multiple nuclei simultaneously.

Key shift: Electrons belong to the whole molecule, not a single bond.
2

LCAO & Conservation of Orbitals

MOs are approximated via Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals (LCAO)—adding and subtracting atomic wavefunctions (ψA ± ψB).

N Atomic Orbitals → N Molecular Orbitals
Orbitals are never created or destroyed—only combined!
3

Energy & Symmetry Matching

Atomic orbitals combine effectively only when two conditions are met:

  • ✓ Similar Energy: 1s mixes with 1s, not 2s (unless close in energy like 2s–2p).
  • ✓ Compatible Symmetry: Wave lobes must align with matching symmetry along the internuclear axis.
Orthogonal orbitals (e.g., s + px) have zero net overlap.
Principle 4 • Quantum Wave Interference

Bonding vs. Antibonding Molecular Orbitals

Electrons behave as standing waves with mathematical phases (+ and −). Combining two AOs yields one lower-energy bonding MO and one higher-energy antibonding MO.

(+) Wave Phase (−) Wave Phase

Head-On Overlap: σ and σ* Orbitals (from 1s or 2pz)

Cylindrical Symmetry

Side-by-Side Overlap: π and π* Orbitals (from 2px, 2py)

2 Degenerate Sets
Principle 5 • Populating MOs

The Same 3 Quantum Filling Rules

  • 1. Aufbau Principle: Fill MOs from lowest energy to highest energy.
  • 2. Pauli Exclusion: Max 2 electrons per MO, with opposite spins (↑↓).
  • 3. Hund's Rule: Place 1 electron with parallel spin (↑ ↑) in each degenerate MO (like π2p) before pairing.
Bond Order Formula
BO = ½ (Bonding e− − Antibonding e−)

BO > 0 → Stable molecule  |  BO = 0 → Does not exist
Higher BO = Shorter & Stronger bond

Magnetic Behavior
Paramagnetic: ≥ 1 unpaired electron (↑). Attracted into a magnetic field.
Diamagnetic: All electrons paired (↑↓). Weakly repelled by a magnetic field.

Guided Walkthroughs

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Follow the 4-step MO problem-solving method: (1) Count valence e−, (2) Pick energy ordering, (3) Fill MOs via Aufbau/Hund, (4) Compute Bond Order & Magnetism.

Worked Example 1 Period 1

Why Does H2 Exist While He2 Does Not?

Step 1 (Valence e−):
• H2: 1 + 1 = 2 valence e−
• He2: 2 + 2 = 4 valence e−

Step 2 & 3 (MO Filling):
Two 1s AOs combine to form σ1s (bonding) and σ*1s (antibonding).
• H2: (σ1s)2
• He2: (σ1s)2(σ*1s)2

Step 4 (Bond Order & Conclusion):
• BO(H2) = ½(2 − 0) = 1.0 (Single bond, diamagnetic, stable).
• BO(He2) = ½(2 − 2) = 0. Because antibonding destabilization slightly exceeds bonding stabilization, He2 has zero net bond and falls apart!

Worked Example 2 Z ≥ 8

Solving the O2 Paramagnetism Paradox

Step 1 (Valence e−):
Each O atom (Group 6A) has 6 valence e− → 12 valence e− total.

Step 2 (Ordering for Z ≥ 8):
Large 2s–2p gap means σ2p lies below π2p.

Step 3 (Fill 12 e−):
(σ2s)2(σ*2s)2(σ2p)2(π2p)4(π*2p)2
By Hund's Rule, the last 2 electrons enter the two degenerate π*2p orbitals unpaired with parallel spins (↑   ↑).

Step 4 (Bond Order & Magnetism):
• BO = ½(8 − 4) = 2.0 (matches O=O double bond).
• 2 unpaired e− → Paramagnetic! MO theory explains both the double bond and magnetism simultaneously.

Worked Example 3 Z ≤ 7

Comparing N2 vs. N2+ Bond Strength

Step 1 (Valence e−):
• N2: 5 + 5 = 10 valence e−
• N2+: 10 − 1 = 9 valence e−

Step 2 & 3 (Z ≤ 7 Ordering with s–p mixing):
Here π2p is below σ2p.
• N2: (σ2s)2(σ*2s)2(π2p)4(σ2p)2
• N2+: removes 1 e− from the HOMO (σ2p) → (σ2p)1

Step 4 (Comparison):
• BO(N2) = ½(8 − 2) = 3.0 (diamagnetic, 110 pm).
• BO(N2+) = ½(7 − 2) = 2.5 (paramagnetic, 112 pm).
Removing a bonding electron weakens and lengthens the bond!
